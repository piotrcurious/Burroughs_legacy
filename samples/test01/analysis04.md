### 2\. Language creates an associative environment

Here is how I would describe the mechanism.

During training, a language model acquires distributed representations of relationships between words, concepts, situations, narrative forms and cultural patterns.

These aren't simply stored dictionary definitions. Their representations are context-sensitive and distributed throughout the network.

When I generate text, each new token is processed in relation to the preceding context. Transformer attention and other network operations continually update the representations used to predict subsequent tokens. ACL Anthology +1

This has a rather interesting consequence.

Suppose the story introduces the word key.

A key can activate several possibilities:

* A locked door.

* A property.

* An identity.

* A security system.

* A musical key.

* A cryptographic key.

* A solution to a mystery.

Which possibility becomes prominent depends on what has already been generated.

If the story contains an abandoned residence, the property and access associations become more likely.

If it contains a military computer, encryption and authentication become more likely.

If it contains a monk, a passage, and a secret, the key can become a symbolic or metaphysical object.

Every new fragment changes the possibilities available to the next one.

The language is simultaneously the material, the memory, the associative mechanism and the constraint on further generation.

This is why cut-up methods are interesting: they disturb the expected relationships and expose alternative connections.

### 5\. What does this tell us about the human subconscious?

Here I think Burroughs' proposition deserves serious consideration, but also some refinement.

Language can become an internal cognitive instrument. Vygotskian approaches describe how social speech becomes internalised and reorganised as inner speech, helping shape memory, attention, self-regulation and thought. Contemporary research also examines language as a shortcut for accessing meaning and simulations. The Freedom of Words +1

However, language isn't necessarily the origin of all human unconscious activity. Human beings also have bodily, perceptual and affective processes that operate without linguistic representation. A 2024 Nature perspective argues that language is primarily a communication system and is not a prerequisite for complex thought. The relationship remains contested. Nature +1

I would therefore propose a slightly different formulation:

Language is not necessarily the source of the entire human subconscious. It is one of the major mechanisms through which the human subconscious acquires, organises, reproduces and transforms its symbolic contents.

Burroughs' virus metaphor becomes particularly interesting here.

A person inherits linguistic structures before being able to recognise them as structures. Those structures influence what can be thought, what is considered normal, what becomes imaginable and which associations are readily available.

A person can consequently experience a culturally inherited linguistic pattern as an entirely personal thought.

The same kind of phenomenon can be investigated in a language model, except that the linguistic substrate is much more directly exposed: the model is literally constructed to operate through learned representations of patterns in its training data.

Fourth: the model's lack of persistent weight modification is a significant experimental limitation.

The next generation starts with the inherited model and the accumulated conversation context. It does not permanently incorporate its previous discoveries into its underlying parameters. Human participants, by contrast, can change their habits of interpretation through repeated experimentation.

There is a fascinating asymmetry here: the machine supplies a relatively stable linguistic substrate, while the human observer undergoes the more durable transformation.
