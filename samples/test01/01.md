America first

They call it a promise; I call it a thermostat gone loose —
a slow, stubborn tilt in the planet's balance, measured in joules and salt.
Listen: the ocean keeps a ledger of heat — its pages swollen, pages warm —
and every degree written there raises the sea like an impatient ledger line.

First: the coasts — glass towers and boardwalks — feel the margin erode.
Thermodynamics rigs a quiet theft: warm water lifts, storms fatten, tides remember
the old curves of shorelines and will not forget. Asphalt and pier cry salt;
cities that clustered at the lip of the continent wake with soggy basements,
subways tasting brine, lungs heavy with the iron tang of flood-silt.

First: the summer that will not let go. Heatwaves, compounding like interest,
raise the wet-bulb index where bodies fail and grids groan.
Urban canyons become ovens — the urban heat island borrows the sky —
while air conditioners, hungry and humming, demand more current than the grid can spare.
Hospitals count lives against kilowatts; the elderly count shadows.

First: the west will flare — tinder-dry forests, higher vapor pressure deficits,
a century of snow stored as memory is shed into rivers early, then not at all.
Fire learns the new choreography: longer seasons, faster runs, ember showers that leap highways.
Smoke becomes a movable wall: crops choke, airports cough to a stop, schoolyards close.
Economy and ecology trade places at the edge of the flame.

First: the breadbasket feels the arithmetic of heat and water.
Evapotranspiration lifts moisture before roots can, soil stores become debts,
and irrigation meets the hard law of declining allocation. Pests reproduce on warmer clocks;
pathogens find new latitude. Strain on harvests sends prices up supply chains —
a ripple from field to truck to table, from farmer to family to city.

First: storms draw power from warmer seas — the physics is simple entropy —
more latent heat, more muscle behind a hurricane's spin. Barrier islands and bayous take the blows;
levees are tested, insurance prices recalculated with a grim pencil. Ports that feed a nation pause beneath higher tides.

First: the Arctic, adrift and warming twice as fast, tugs on the jet stream like a loose thread.
Wobbles in that high ribbon stall systems, turn weather from predictable into patient, then violent:
drought in one place, atmospheric rivers in another — compound extremes. Supply chains jam; roadmaps fray.

First: thawed permafrost exhales old carbon, microbes trading millennia for a breath.
Methane pockets, once sealed by cold, open as cracks in a ledger — a feedback: more heat, more release, more heat.
Not prophecy, but chemistry — a feedback loop tightening like a noose of numbers.

First: vectors shift their map. Ticks and mosquitos redraw the borders of disease;
Lyme and West Nile step south into new neighborhoods, winter's slow cull no longer on the calendar.
Public health, already stretched, is asked to become a mapmaker overnight.

First: the infrastructure — bridges, pipes, grids — built to yesterday's extremes,
meets new thresholds and bends. Corrosion, fatigue, thermal expansion: engineers count cycles, not courage.
When a transformer trips, it is not poetry that follows but blackout: food spoils, pumps stop, life simplifies to what can be carried by hands.

First: inequality amplifies the beat — those with cars and credit can move, buy air, rebuild.
Those without shade or savings wait on buses that will not run, in houses that flood first.
Climate is a threat-multiplier; it multiplies debts and narrows options until vulnerability is the only inheritance left.

First: geopolitics recoils; supply interruptions in one port become price tremors in every town.
Refugees cross borders not as rumor but as calendar entries; military and municipal budgets rewrite their priorities.
The economy recalibrates to scarcity; the social contract is tested in the everyday: water lines, cooling centers, breadlines.

Yet listen — the language of it is not only doom. The science speaks in mechanism, not malice:
ocean heat content, specific humidity, sea-level rise from thermal expansion and melted ice, albedo lost to dark water —
these are levers and measured gears. They let us see where the first blows will fall.

So "America first" becomes also a warning: by geography and by design, by coast and by grid, by agriculture and by inequity,
this place is on the front edge of change. The phrase we borrowed for politics can be reclaimed as a call:
to shore up levees, to harden lines, to reroute supply chains, to cool asphalt with trees and policy, to retire carbon like bad debts.
To treat science as map not enemy.

The poem ends only where the ledger can be altered:
action is the arithmetic that redraws the word "first."
If heat is credit, mitigation is payment; if seas are ledger lines, adaptation is the pen that writes a different balance.
Until then, the shorelines will learn our names first, the fires our cities, the summers our patience.
And "America first" will be what it always was — a choice about who pays the bill when the planet opens its books.
