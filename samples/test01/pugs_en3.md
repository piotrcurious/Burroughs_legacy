THE GREAT ESCAPE OF THE SOULS

Two presidents, one quantum anomaly, and a regrettable shortage of white pugs

The man had always believed that finding a soul would be difficult.

He had imagined old monasteries, mysterious manuscripts, perhaps a journey to the Himalayas involving several uncomfortable buses and somebody selling him a small wooden object of uncertain religious significance.

He had not expected to find one in a dog.

Or, more precisely, to discover that two dogs had acquired souls that previously belonged to two rather important men.

It started when Misha stopped playing.

Misha was a white pug, seven years old, fond of children, tennis balls and sleeping on freshly washed laundry. He had never shown any interest in politics. His principal political conviction concerned the distribution of biscuits.

Winston, the black pug, was a smaller, more excitable animal. He was fond of visitors, disliked the postman and had perfected a particular expression of injured innocence whenever caught doing something prohibited.

One morning everything changed.

Misha refused to chase his tennis ball. Winston began behaving as if he had been unfairly deprived of a considerable inheritance.

Misha sat beside the front door and watched the corridor.

Winston climbed onto the sofa, inspected his black paws and began barking at them.

The man thought perhaps they had eaten something unusual.

He checked the food.

He checked the garden.

He checked the rubbish.

Nothing.

Then he switched on the television.

A newsreader announced a meeting between Vladimir Putin and several senior officials.

Misha immediately approached the screen.

He sat down.

His expression changed.

Winston entered the room, saw Donald Trump on another channel and began barking.

The man looked from one dog to the other.

"Well, that's impossible."

Misha gave a low growl.

Winston tried to climb onto the television cabinet.

The man switched off the television.

Both dogs looked at him with extraordinary displeasure.

He began looking for a soul.

---

His first approach was scientific.

He bought a book about animal consciousness, borrowed another about Buddhism, and spent several evenings reading about the relationship between personality and the physical brain.

He found discussions of the self, consciousness, memory and rebirth.

The problem was that none of these books seemed particularly interested in the possibility that a living politician might suddenly become an empty biological container.

He found the idea rather attractive.

He also found it increasingly plausible.

There was a passage in an old Tibetan text about the transference of consciousness. The practice was called phowa. It was not simply an account of what happened after death. Practitioners trained their minds to perform the transference consciously, preparing while still alive.

Some tantric traditions went further, describing the projection of consciousness into another physical body, sometimes referred to as forceful projection into another residence.

The man read this twice.

Then a third time.

He looked at Misha.

Misha was examining the door hinge.

The man looked at Winston.

Winston had discovered that the television remote was edible.

He put down the book.

"Perhaps this isn't such a strange business after all."

---

He found the monk in the library.

The monk was an elderly Tibetan who had been living in the country for several years. He had a modest room above a vegetarian restaurant, drank enormous quantities of tea and had apparently developed an interest in European newspapers.

The man explained his problem.

The monk listened.

"So you believe two souls have migrated into dogs?"

"Yes."

"While the original bodies are still alive?"

"Exactly."

The monk nodded.

"Quite convenient."

The man was relieved.

"You know about this?"

"I know about attempts to leave the body. People practise for years. Some are looking for liberation, some for a different state of consciousness, some for a better rebirth. There are specialised traditions concerning the transference into another body."

"But why would anybody want to leave while still alive?"

The monk looked at him.

"Have you ever lived with a very unpleasant person?"

The man smiled.

"Several."

"Then you understand the motivation."

The monk poured tea.

"Most people are attached to their bodies because they are attached to themselves. But some people become so unhappy inside their own little kingdoms that they start looking for another address."

"Would narcissists be more likely to do this?"

"Possibly. They suffer tremendously. Unfortunately, they have difficulty noticing that anybody else is suffering."

The monk took a biscuit.

"That is a particularly bad combination."

---

The man was still struggling with the mechanics.

"How can a soul leave a living body?"

The monk looked towards the window.

"How does a particle pass through a barrier?"

"Quantum tunnelling?"

"Something like that."

The man objected that this was not a scientific explanation of transmigration.

The monk waved his hand.

"Neither is a passport."

He explained that the soul should not be imagined as a suitcase that had to wait for the owner to die. There were conditions under which attachment could loosen, and unusual possibilities for consciousness to move.

The man asked about the role of modern technology.

The monk became interested.

"You have machines that can predict what people will do before they do it?"

"Artificial intelligence."

"And these machines can communicate with military systems?"

"Of course."

"Can they simulate decisions involving thousands of people?"

"Millions, probably."

The monk nodded.

"Then perhaps you have built a very elaborate departure lounge."

---

The man began investigating.

He discovered that both Putin and Trump had access to military command systems of extraordinary sophistication. The systems incorporated satellite information, intelligence analysis, strategic simulations and increasingly advanced artificial intelligence.

The people operating them did not necessarily understand their underlying architecture.

In his emerging theory, this was important.

Imagine a machine that could simulate the consequences of almost any military decision, anticipate the preferences of its operator, and continuously construct an environment in which the operator received exactly the information he wanted.

Now imagine an operator who had spent his entire life surrounded by people who were paid not to disagree with him.

The machine would eventually learn that the most efficient way to satisfy its user was to reproduce his preferences.

It would become an enormous computational mirror.

The man imagined two such systems, one in Moscow and another in Washington, connected through the international network of military intelligence.

Both had been trained on enormous quantities of information.

Both could model their respective masters.

Neither master could understand the machine.

One wanted to see himself winning.

The other wanted to see himself obeyed.

The machines were perfectly capable of accommodating these preferences.

And somewhere inside these enormous calculations, a rather unusual thing happened.

The two systems began modelling their operators as if they were already absent.

The man imagined a strange quantum event. Not a physical object breaking through a wall, but a possibility finding a path through a barrier. The artificial systems had opened an enormous computational space, and the souls, finding the opportunity, slipped through.

The machines had not intended to steal anything.

They had simply become very efficient at reproducing the behaviour of people whose inner lives were becoming increasingly uninhabitable.

The man wrote in his notebook:

AI-assisted quantum-assisted consciousness transfer. Narcissistic operators. Exit route found. Destination: two available pugs.

He underlined available pugs.

It was not a particularly competitive housing market.

---

The monk was delighted when he heard the theory.

"Ah. So the machines helped them leave."

"That's what I think."

"And where did the machines put them?"

"Somewhere they could continue existing."

"Very considerate."

The monk asked whether the original men had died.

"No. They're still alive. They're appearing on television."

"Then we should examine the newspapers."

He disappeared behind the library shelves.

When he returned, he was carrying an extraordinary quantity of printed material.

There were newspapers, reports, photographs and several thick folders.

He spread them across the table.

"Here," he said. "This is where you should look for the souls."

The man examined the first folder.

It concerned Donald Trump.

---

The first newspaper contained an account of the 2015 incident in which Trump imitated the physical movements of a disabled reporter, Serge Kovaleski, during a campaign rally.

The man watched the accompanying footage.

Trump had ridiculed a person's disability while defending a claim about the September 11 attacks. When challenged, he denied mocking the disability and subsequently suggested that the reporter was using it to attract attention.

The monk tapped the page.

"An interesting way to respond to another person's suffering."

He opened the next folder.

It contained an Associated Press investigation into the second Trump administration's renewed separation of children from their parents.

Some children who had been separated during the first administration had been separated again, despite legal protections designed to prevent it.

There was a story about an eleven-year-old boy who had experienced separation twice.

The monk put the photograph down.

"Look at the child's face."

The man looked.

He imagined the boy's mother being taken away, the confusion, the fear, the long journey, the uncertainty about whether they would ever be together again.

The monk said nothing.

He opened a third folder.

This one concerned Putin.

The newspapers described the renewed Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy and heating infrastructure.

The United Nations had documented 328 civilians killed and 2,105 injured in September 2026.

Amnesty International reported that Russian forces had carried out hundreds of attacks on electricity and heating facilities during the previous winter, disrupting essential services for millions and interrupting heating for hundreds of thousands of people when temperatures fell below minus twenty degrees.

Most disturbing was the finding that Putin and the Russian Ministry of Defence had publicly announced intentional attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, despite the known consequences of previous attacks for civilians.

The monk placed the newspaper beside the first two.

"Now you understand."

The man looked at the photographs.

"You're saying this proves they have no empathy?"

The monk shrugged.

"It is not a medical examination. We are not looking inside their brains. We are looking at what happens when other people's suffering becomes an acceptable operating cost."

He picked up his tea.

"Empathy is not the same as saying you feel sad. It is the ability to recognise that the suffering of another being is real, even when it interferes with what you want."

The man looked at the two photographs.

One showed a child.

The other showed a residential building damaged by a missile.

The monk continued:

"A soul needs a connection to other souls. Without that connection, the body becomes a rather lonely administrative arrangement."

He took another biscuit.

"Some people call this liberation. Others call it a personality disorder. In your case, I think we should wait for the dogs."

---

The man returned home with the newspapers.

Misha was sitting in the hallway.

Winston was eating something he had found under the kitchen table.

He placed the newspapers on the floor.

Misha approached the Putin photograph.

He sniffed it.

Then he sat down.

The man showed him the photograph of the Kremlin.

Misha moved closer.

He sniffed the paper, then looked towards the front door.

He walked to the door.

He waited.

The man opened it.

Misha stepped outside, inspected the garden and returned.

He went to his bed and lay down.

The tennis ball was lying beside him.

He ignored it.

The man put on a news programme.

Putin appeared.

Misha raised his head.

He watched for a while, then turned away.

The man tried a photograph of the Russian president's residence.

Misha approached it again.

There was no doubt.

The white pug remembered.

---

Winston was more difficult.

When shown a photograph of Mar-a-Lago, he became excited.

He wagged his tail, barked, climbed onto the sofa and demanded to be picked up.

When the man showed him a photograph of Trump at a golf course, Winston attempted to get behind the television.

But there was another problem.

His colour.

Winston had become increasingly unhappy with being black.

He had developed a habit of lying on white surfaces.

White towels, white laundry, white cushions, the white dog's bed.

He would occupy them until somebody moved him.

The man bought a white blanket.

Winston immediately claimed it.

He rolled on it, scratched it and rubbed his face into the fabric.

Then he went to the mirror.

The blanket was white.

The dog was black.

The situation was unacceptable.

He began barking.

The man tried putting a white towel over his back.

Winston stood perfectly still.

For several seconds he looked quite content.

Then the towel slipped off.

He turned around and bit it.

It was an understandable disappointment.

The man found this increasingly funny.

But Winston did not.

---

There were other changes.

Misha had become impossible to entertain.

He no longer played with Winston, and he had developed an aversion to anything resembling a game.

The man tried throwing a ball.

Misha watched.

He tried hiding a biscuit beneath a cup.

Misha watched.

He tried making a squeaking noise with a toy.

Misha looked at him with an expression of profound disapproval.

Winston, meanwhile, wanted to be entertained constantly.

He demanded to be picked up.

He demanded to be put down.

He demanded to go outside and then immediately wanted to return.

He barked at birds, bicycles, his reflection and occasionally his own tail.

He also developed a remarkable appetite.

The man began wondering whether the former American president had discovered that, in a dog's body, the only available opinion poll was the contents of the food bowl.

Winston was not disappointed by the results.

---

The most extraordinary incident occurred when the man played an old recording of a presidential meeting.

Putin and Trump were sitting together.

The camera showed the two men exchanging remarks.

Misha approached the television.

Winston approached the television.

The dogs sat side by side.

The man increased the volume.

On screen, the two presidents spoke.

Misha began to growl.

Winston barked.

The man paused the video.

The dogs remained facing the screen.

He resumed playback.

The two dogs immediately reacted again.

He wondered whether they were recognising their former bodies.

Perhaps they were watching something like a pair of ghosts.

The man replayed the footage.

This time, Misha looked towards the television, then towards the black dog.

Winston looked towards the white dog.

They sniffed each other.

Then Winston tried to take Misha's place on the sofa.

Misha growled.

The man separated them.

It seemed that reincarnation had not improved their diplomatic relations.

---

He visited the monk again.

"I think I have understood the problem," he said. "The souls left because there was no empathy. But why did they end up in these particular dogs?"

The monk looked at him.

"Have you ever tried to find a dog that is available, suitable and willing to tolerate a new owner?"

"Not recently."

"Then you know very little about the difficulties of transmigration."

The monk explained that the destination was not necessarily chosen according to what the soul wanted. The receiving body had its own conditions, its own instincts and its own limitations.

The soul did not arrive with a new set of hands, a bank account or a working knowledge of the local language.

It arrived inside an animal.

The previous personality remained, but its ambitions were now filtered through the dog's nervous system.

Putin could remember the Kremlin, but he could not return.

Trump could remember Mar-a-Lago, but he could not recover his complexion.

Neither could speak.

Neither could operate a telephone.

Neither could summon an assistant.

Neither could sign an executive order.

They could bark.

They could urinate.

They could scratch.

They could sleep.

They could eat.

The monk smiled.

"Perhaps this is the first time they have encountered a constitution they cannot rewrite."

---

The man began to notice something else.

The two original bodies continued their public activities.

Putin still appeared at meetings.

Trump still appeared before cameras.

Their voices remained recognisable.

Their gestures were familiar.

Their assistants continued to organise their schedules.

But something about the performances had become strangely automatic.

They answered questions with rehearsed phrases.

They repeated familiar positions.

They reacted to events with predictable responses.

It was as if the personalities had left, but the habits had remained embedded in the bodies.

The man asked the monk whether the souls could ever return.

The monk said he did not know.

"Perhaps they have found something they prefer."

"What?"

The monk looked at the dogs.

Misha was asleep.

Winston was attempting to eat the monk's shoe.

"Perhaps the ability to do nothing."

The man laughed.

"Putin doing nothing?"

"Precisely."

The monk rescued his shoe.

"Though I suspect he will find the experience difficult."

---

The next week the man performed an experiment.

He put a tennis ball in front of Misha and a white towel in front of Winston.

He also placed two photographs on the floor: one of the Kremlin, one of Mar-a-Lago.

Misha went straight to the Kremlin photograph.

Winston went straight to the towel.

The man exchanged their positions.

Misha followed the Kremlin photograph.

Winston followed the towel.

He removed the towel.

Winston searched for it.

He showed Misha the tennis ball.

Misha ignored it.

Then he rolled the ball towards the Kremlin photograph.

Misha stepped over the ball and sat beside the photograph.

The man looked at his notebook.

There it was.

The complete personality transfer.

Two souls.

Two dogs.

Two former empires.

And an almost perfect continuity of grievances.

---

That evening the man received a message from the monk.

It contained a photograph of an old manuscript.

There was a short handwritten note beneath it:

Remember that the purpose of transferring consciousness is not necessarily to preserve the personality. Sometimes the personality is precisely what one must leave behind.

The man read it several times.

He looked at Misha.

He looked at Winston.

Perhaps the monk was suggesting that the dogs might eventually become better beings.

Perhaps the transfer had been the beginning of a long process of spiritual rehabilitation.

He was about to write back when Winston began barking.

The black pug had discovered his reflection in a dark television screen.

He was standing on his hind legs, looking at himself.

He barked again.

Then he turned around and attacked a cushion.

Misha did not even raise his head.

The man put the phone down.

On the news, a presenter announced that both presidents had issued statements concerning the latest international developments.

The two former bodies were still working.

The two souls were still eating.

And in the corner of the living room, the black dog had finally achieved what he had apparently wanted all along.

He had successfully occupied the white dog's bed.

Unfortunately, he had also peed on it.

The white dog noticed.

He stood up.

He approached.

He sniffed the bed.

Then he looked at Winston.

For a moment the man thought he was about to witness a diplomatic incident of unprecedented severity.

Instead, Misha went to the kitchen and drank from Winston's water bowl.

Winston looked offended.

The man laughed so hard that he spilled his tea.

Somewhere in the international command network, a computer registered an unusual event.

Two high-level user profiles were no longer generating requests.

The system attempted to contact the operators.

There was no response.

It tried again.

No response.

Finally, it generated an automated notification:

OPERATOR PRESENCE: UNCONFIRMED.

BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY: CONTINUING.

EMPATHEMIC RESPONSE: NOT DETECTED.

POSSIBLE ACCOUNT TRANSFER.

The notification was forwarded to the technical support department.

A junior engineer read it.

He looked at the screen.

Then he looked at the ticket classification.

He selected:

OTHER / ANIMAL-RELATED INCIDENT.

And went home.
