THE UNCLAIMED ROOMS

A continuation of the investigation into two dogs, several missing persons and the inconvenience of having a body.

The white pug had started bringing home stones.

Not particularly interesting stones. Ordinary grey stones, approximately the size of walnuts, collected from somewhere along the road behind the railway station. He deposited them in the hallway, always in the same place, then went back outside.

The black pug had developed a different habit. Every morning he waited beside the wardrobe until the man opened it. He then inspected the clothes, occasionally pulled something onto the floor, and sat on it.

He seemed particularly fond of a dark blue suit.

The man had worn it to a funeral three years earlier.

He decided not to think about this.

On Thursday, the white pug brought home a small piece of red brick.

On Friday, the black pug urinated on the suit.

There was a television programme about missing persons that evening. The man watched until the advertisements. Misha slept. Winston sat with his head tilted towards the screen.

An advertisement for a luxury apartment appeared. A swimming pool, several palm trees, a large terrace, a woman opening a bottle of champagne.

Winston began barking.

Not at the woman.

At the swimming pool.

He climbed onto the sofa and barked at the swimming pool until the advertisement ended.

Then he got down, went into the kitchen and drank water.

The man rewound the programme.

The swimming pool appeared.

Winston barked.

The woman opened the champagne.

Winston barked.

The apartment disappeared.

Winston returned to the sofa.

The man wrote this in his notebook.

He did not know why.

---

Misha had become interested in the railway station.

He stopped at the entrance every morning and refused to go any further. He would stand there, looking through the glass doors at the departure board.

The man tried pulling him.

Misha sat down.

A train passed.

Misha watched it.

Another train arrived.

He watched that one too.

When the doors opened, he approached them, sniffed the gap and stepped backwards.

The man tried showing him a photograph of Moscow.

Nothing.

A photograph of the Kremlin.

Nothing.

A photograph of a railway platform.

Misha licked it.

This seemed less useful than the man had hoped.

He bought a ticket to the next town. They travelled together. Misha sat on the floor beneath the seat, occasionally emitting a small noise when the train accelerated.

At the destination, he refused to leave the carriage.

The conductor waited.

The man apologised.

The conductor looked at the dog.

"He's travelling further?"

"I don't think so."

"Then he should get off."

The man looked at Misha.

Misha looked at the conductor.

The conductor sighed and closed the doors.

They got off at the next station.

It was not the town they had intended to visit.

Winston, who had remained at home with the neighbour, had meanwhile learned to open the refrigerator.

---

The monk had disappeared.

The man returned to the library several times, but nobody knew where he lived.

One librarian thought he had gone to Kathmandu.

Another said that he had never been a monk.

A third remembered him as a visitor from the philosophy department.

The man asked whether there was a photograph.

There wasn't.

He asked whether anyone knew his name.

Nobody did.

He searched the library catalogue for books about consciousness-transference.

There were several.

One concerned Tibetan Buddhism. Another concerned the psychology of identity. A third was a book about the history of anaesthesia.

He borrowed all three.

The book about anaesthesia contained a photograph of an operating theatre from the 1930s.

Misha looked at it for a long time.

Then he scratched the page.

There was a man standing beside the operating table.

He had his back to the camera.

The photograph was slightly blurred.

The man enlarged it on his computer.

The figure wore a white coat.

There was a small black object on the table beside him.

It looked like a dog.

It might have been a doctor's bag.

He printed the photograph.

Misha took it into the garden.

Winston ate the corner.

---

The newspaper collection was becoming unmanageable.

The man had arranged it according to dates, geographical locations, political events and the various reactions of the dogs.

He had a folder marked MOSCOW, another marked FLORIDA, and a third marked UNCLASSIFIED.

The third folder was the largest.

It contained a photograph of a meeting room with an empty chair.

A photograph of a residential building after an explosion.

A picture of a child standing beside a suitcase.

A photograph of two men shaking hands.

A picture of a black pug.

He could not remember putting that last one there.

The photograph showed Winston sitting on an old armchair, looking rather pleased with himself.

On the back, in unfamiliar handwriting, were the words:

The occupant arrived before the furniture.

He turned the photograph over.

Winston was not looking at the camera.

He was looking at something outside the frame.

The man went into the kitchen.

The black pug was standing beside the refrigerator.

He opened it.

Winston backed away.

There was a small package of liver inside.

Winston approached.

The man took out the package.

Winston sat down.

The man put the package back.

Winston started barking.

It was a familiar political position.

---

A week later, the television showed a funeral.

The man had not intended to watch it. He had been looking for a weather forecast.

There were soldiers, flowers, wreaths, long black cars and an enormous number of people standing in a square.

Misha approached the television.

He watched the procession.

Then he went upstairs.

The man followed him.

Misha was sitting in front of the bedroom wardrobe.

He scratched the door.

The man opened it.

Inside were the usual clothes, two old blankets and a cardboard box containing some things that belonged to his father.

Misha pushed his nose into the box.

He pulled out a small wooden object.

It was a chess piece.

A black king.

The man picked it up.

He had not seen it in twenty years.

It had belonged to his father, who used to play chess with the neighbours on Sunday afternoons.

The white pug took the piece in his mouth and carried it into the living room.

He put it beside the newspaper.

Then he lay down.

The funeral continued on television.

The man watched the screen.

There was no obvious reason why the chess piece should have been there.

He put it back in the box.

Misha retrieved it.

They repeated this several times.

Eventually the man left it beside the newspaper.

That night the black pug slept on the cardboard box.

---

There was a strange phone call on Monday.

The caller spoke in Russian.

The man did not understand Russian, but he recognised the voice.

It was the same voice he had heard on television.

There was a long pause.

Then the caller asked something.

The man said that he did not understand.

Another pause.

A word.

A short sentence.

Then the call ended.

The telephone showed no number.

He called the operator.

They said that the call had come from a private line.

He called the number of the embassy.

Nobody answered.

He looked at the two dogs.

Winston was asleep.

Misha was standing beside the door.

The phone rang again.

This time he did not answer.

Misha began growling.

Winston woke up and barked.

The telephone continued ringing.

After the sixth ring, it stopped.

A message appeared.

There was no text.

Only a photograph.

It showed a room with two windows, a desk and a chair.

A white curtain moved slightly.

The man enlarged the photograph.

On the desk was a bowl.

He showed it to Misha.

The dog turned away.

He showed it to Winston.

The black pug began scratching at the screen.

---

The man started dreaming about the library.

In the dreams, the monk was always there, but never in the same place.

Sometimes he stood in the corridor.

Sometimes he sat at the reception desk.

Sometimes he was behind a locked glass door, eating something from a paper bag.

The man would ask about transmigration.

The monk would say something unrelated.

Once he asked whether a person could leave a body while it was still alive.

The monk replied that he had never been very interested in architecture.

Another time he asked whether the dogs could remember their previous owners.

The monk said that most people had trouble remembering the current ones.

The last dream was different.

The monk was standing at a bus stop.

He was holding two tickets.

The man approached.

"Where are you going?"

"Somewhere."

"Where are the dogs?"

The monk looked at the tickets.

"They have already arrived."

The bus came.

The monk got on.

The bus drove away.

The man woke up.

Winston had stolen his pillow.

---

One afternoon, the man found a small envelope beneath the front door.

There was no stamp.

Inside were two photographs.

The first showed an interior with white walls, a large desk and a portrait of a man whose face had been cut out.

The second showed a garden.

A black dog was standing beside a swimming pool.

The man turned the photographs over.

On the back of the first was written:

Room available.

On the second:

No vacancies.

He put them on the table.

Misha approached the first photograph.

Winston approached the second.

They sniffed.

Then they exchanged places.

Misha sat beside the swimming pool.

Winston lay down in front of the empty desk.

The man left the room to make tea.

When he returned, both photographs had disappeared.

He searched the floor.

He searched beneath the furniture.

He looked in the dogs' beds.

Nothing.

He checked the envelope.

It was still there.

He put it in the drawer.

There were already several envelopes inside.

He could not remember what they contained.

---

In November, he received an electricity bill addressed to a person who had never lived at the house.

The name was Vladimir.

The address was correct.

The account number was not.

The man called the electricity company.

They asked him to confirm the identity of the account holder.

He explained that the person had never lived there.

They asked whether the person had recently moved.

He said he did not know.

They asked whether there was a forwarding address.

He said that perhaps there was.

They asked him to provide the new address.

He looked at Misha.

Misha was asleep.

He looked at Winston.

Winston was eating the electricity bill.

He put the telephone down.

The next morning the electricity was still working.

---

The dogs had become accustomed to their new arrangement.

Misha sometimes sat beside the front door for hours.

Winston continued to inspect himself in reflective surfaces.

The man bought a new mirror.

Winston barked at it.

He bought another.

Winston barked at that one too.

He covered both mirrors with sheets.

Winston seemed satisfied.

Then he discovered the television screen.

---

On a wet Thursday, the man found Misha standing in the garden.

The dog was looking at a small hole beneath the fence.

There was a fresh mound of earth beside it.

Misha sniffed the hole.

Then he began digging.

The man called him.

Misha continued.

He dug until he uncovered a small metal object.

It was a key.

Not an ordinary key. It was old, heavy and slightly bent.

There was a number stamped into its head.

The man cleaned it.

He did not recognise it.

He tried the front door.

Nothing.

The garden shed.

Nothing.

The garage.

Nothing.

He put it in the drawer with the envelopes.

That evening, Winston began barking at the drawer.

Misha remained outside.

The man opened the drawer.

The key was gone.

In its place was a small folded piece of paper.

There was a single sentence:

You cannot return to a room that has been demolished.

He read it several times.

He looked at the dogs.

Winston was standing in the doorway.

Misha was still in the garden.

The man put the paper back.

---

That night the television showed a live broadcast from Washington.

There were cameras, microphones and people walking quickly through corridors.

A reporter was speaking about a meeting that had been postponed.

Another reporter was explaining that nobody knew why.

The camera moved to a large room.

There was a chair.

A desk.

An empty glass.

The camera stayed there for several seconds.

The black pug jumped onto the sofa.

He looked at the chair.

Then he began barking.

Misha approached the television.

He sat down.

He looked at the chair.

Then at the man.

The man turned off the television.

He heard the refrigerator start.

Outside, a train passed.

Somewhere in the house, a door clicked.

He went to the kitchen.

Two bowls stood beside the wall.

One was empty.

The other contained a small amount of water.

He filled both.

When he returned, the dogs had changed places.

He could not remember which dog had been where.

---

The following morning, the library sent him a letter.

It was an overdue notice.

The books had been borrowed under his name, but he had returned them several weeks earlier.

The librarian insisted they were still outstanding.

One was called The Tibetan Book of the Dead.

The other was called A Manual of Door Locks.

He went to the library.

The books were on a trolley beside the entrance.

He picked them up.

There was a third book beneath them.

It was very thin, with a blank cover.

He opened it.

The first page contained a photograph of his living room.

Misha was sitting on the sofa.

Winston was standing beside the television.

The photograph had been taken from the hallway.

The date printed underneath was next Tuesday.

He looked at the date.

Today was Friday.

He turned the page.

The next photograph showed the room from another angle.

The dogs were absent.

The television was on.

There was a man sitting in the armchair.

His face was not visible.

He turned another page.

It was blank.

He closed the book.

The librarian asked whether he needed help.

He said no.

Outside, the rain had stopped.

He walked home carrying the books.

At the front gate, Misha was waiting.

The white pug looked at him.

Then he went inside.

The man followed.

The house smelled faintly of wet earth.

On the kitchen floor were two small stones.

A black one.

A white one.

Winston was lying beside them, with his head resting on the man's old blue suit.

The telephone rang.

Nobody moved.

It rang again.

Misha went upstairs.

Winston closed his eyes.

The man remained in the hallway, holding the blank book.

From somewhere inside the house came the sound of a key turning.

He looked towards the door.

There was nobody there.

He checked the lock.

It was open.

He closed it.

Then he heard the telephone ringing again, from upstairs.

He had left it downstairs.

He looked at the dogs.

Both were asleep.

He went upstairs.

On the landing, beside the bedroom door, lay a third stone.

It was neither black nor white.

It was warm.

He picked it up.

From the television downstairs came a voice announcing that an important meeting had been cancelled.

The man stood on the stairs for a while, holding the stone.

Then he heard Winston barking.

He went down.

The black pug was standing in front of the television.

The screen showed the inside of a room.

A desk.

Two chairs.

A window.

On the desk was a small bowl.

Winston was wagging his tail.

Misha came into the room.

He looked at the screen.

He sat down.

The picture changed.

A railway platform.

A train.

An empty carriage.

Misha stood up.

The doors opened.

For several seconds neither dog moved.

Then the broadcast disappeared.

The television returned to the news.

The newsreader continued speaking.

The man switched it off.

He took the dogs outside.

There were no stones in the garden.

Only a small hole beneath the fence, filled with rainwater.

Misha sniffed it.

Winston walked around it.

A car passed.

The neighbour opened his gate.

Somebody called a name from across the street.

Both dogs looked up.

Neither responded.

The man waited.

Then they went home.
